# Target Market — Freeprop AI

---

## 1. Overview

Freeprop AI serves Indonesian property agents who need to manage listings and produce promotional content without the overhead of a dedicated design, marketing, or ops team. The market splits into two distinct working modes — **solo agents** operating independently within a defined region, and **team/agency agents** coordinating multiple freelance agents under one shared brand and listing database.

Both segments share the same underlying pain: too much time spent on manual, repetitive work (listing entry, content creation, lead follow-up) that pulls focus away from the one activity that actually generates revenue — closing deals.

---

## 2. Persona

### a. Solo Agent

- **Role:** An independent property agent working alone, without a team or agency structure behind them. Handles the full cycle themselves — sourcing listings, promoting them, and talking to buyers.
- **Background:** Typically operates within a specific city or district (region scope), often relying on personal network and word-of-mouth alongside online listings. May have started as a freelance/part-time agent before treating it as a full-time income source.
- **Goals:**
  - Look as professional and credible as a larger team, despite working alone
  - Keep listings up to date without spending hours on manual entry
  - Get promotional content out consistently without hiring a designer or copywriter
  - Respond to buyer inquiries quickly, even outside working hours
- **Pain Points:**
  - Limited time — every hour spent on content or admin is an hour not spent with clients
  - Limited skill/budget for professional visuals, video, and copywriting
  - Leads get missed or delayed because there's no one else to answer WhatsApp
  - Listings from multiple sources (manual + external sites) are hard to keep organized alone
- **Frequency of Use:** High-frequency, short sessions — likely daily, in short bursts (checking new leads, approving content, updating a listing) between client meetings and site visits.

### b. Team Agent / Brand Agency

- **Role:** A team or agency owner (and the freelance agents under them) operating under a shared brand. The owner manages structure and consistency; individual agents execute promotion and client conversations.
- **Background:** Runs or is part of a property agency/team that recruits freelance agents to expand market coverage. Needs a shared identity and shared listing pool so the brand looks consistent regardless of which agent is posting.
- **Goals:**
  - Recruit and onboard freelance agents efficiently
  - Maintain one consistent brand identity and listing database across the whole team
  - Scale promotional content output without a proportional increase in design/marketing headcount
  - Route and track leads across multiple agents without losing accountability
- **Pain Points:**
  - Coordinating many agents leads to inconsistent branding and messaging
  - Listings duplicated or lost when managed across spreadsheets, chat groups, or individual agents' phones
  - Difficult to see which agent is handling which lead, and whether follow-up is happening
  - Onboarding new freelance agents takes time and produces inconsistent quality until they're trained
- **Frequency of Use:** Mixed frequency — the owner/admin checks in frequently (daily monitoring of team activity, listings, leads), while individual agents use it in short, frequent bursts similar to the solo agent pattern.

---

## 3. Shared Traits Across Personas

- **a. Time-poor** — Both personas are field-based and client-facing; time spent on admin, content creation, or manual data entry is time taken directly from revenue-generating activity.
- **b. Distrustful of tools** — Prior experience with generic CRM or marketing software that didn't fit the property workflow makes both personas skeptical of new tools until they see fast, tangible results.
- **c. Value speed** — Both prioritize how quickly they can get from "new listing" or "new lead" to "content posted" or "buyer responded to" — speed is valued over exhaustive customization.
- **d. Frequency of use** — Both personas engage in short, frequent sessions rather than long, infrequent ones, often checked between client meetings, site visits, or while on the move via mobile.

---

## 4. Design Implications

- **Dashboard should solve their actual problem** — The dashboard should surface exactly what a time-poor agent needs to act on right now (new leads, pending content approvals, listings needing attention), not a generic overview of every possible metric.
- **Support at least two densities of information** — A quick-glance, minimal view for fast mobile check-ins between appointments, and a denser, detailed view for admin/owner-level review and planning sessions.
- **Role-based visibility matters** — A solo agent should only see their own listings, leads, and content. A team owner needs team-wide visibility, while individual team agents should default to their own assigned listings/leads, with escalation visibility only where relevant.
- **Numbers must be scannable** — Given short, frequent usage sessions, key figures (new leads, listings synced, content pending approval, follow-ups due) need to be understood at a glance, without requiring the user to interpret charts or dig into detail views.
